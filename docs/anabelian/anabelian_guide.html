Anabelian Reconstruction Algorithms

How inter-universal Teichmüller theory rebuilds fields, curves and functions from nothing but a group （遠アーベル幾何の復元アルゴリズム入門）

Sources: the papers in pdf/ · numbers from src/iut and from the worked examples in scripts/anabelian/build.js · November 2026
{{fig:mast}}
Abstract   Anabelian geometry asks how much of a variety you can recover from its étale fundamental group alone. Mochizuki's mono-anabelian version asks for more than a yes: it asks for an algorithm whose only input is one abstract profinite group, and whose output is the field, the curve and its functions, with addition and multiplication. Inter-universal Teichmüller theory (IUT) needs these algorithms because its Θ-link glues two universes along groups and monoids but never along rings, so the only way to talk about the ring on the far side is to rebuild it from the shared group. This guide walks through the algorithms one at a time: the local invariants of a Galois group, Uchida's addition-from-multiplication lemma, cyclotomes and Kummer theory, Belyi and elliptic cuspidalization, the full reconstruction theorem [AbsTopIII, Thm 1.9], the archimedean version, the étale-like / Frobenius-like dichotomy and mono-anabelian transport, the three kinds of cyclotomic rigidity, and log-shells. Each one comes with a picture, a worked example whose numbers are computed by the build script, an analogy, and at least one joke. No mathematical claim rests on a joke.

0Why IUT needs reconstruction algorithmsなぜ復元が必要か

Here is the problem in its smallest form, taken from Mochizuki's 2023 Oberwolfach report [Gateway, §2]. Let \(R\) be an integral domain, say \(\mathbb{Z}\), acted on by a group \(G\), and let \(N\ge 2\). The \(N\)-th power map \(x\mapsto x^N\) is an isomorphism of multiplicative monoids from \(R^{\rhd}=R\setminus\{0\}\) onto its image, and it respects the \(G\)-action, but it is not a ring map, because \((x+y)^N\neq x^N+y^N\). Now take two labelled copies \({}^\dagger R\) and \({}^\ddagger R\) and glue them along it:

\[ {}^\dagger G \curvearrowright {}^\dagger R \;\supseteq\; ({}^\dagger R^{\rhd})^N \;\xleftarrow{\;\sim\;}\; {}^\ddagger R^{\rhd} \;\subseteq\; {}^\ddagger R \curvearrowleft {}^\ddagger G. \]

The groups \({}^\dagger G\) and \({}^\ddagger G\) can be identified, and so can the monoids, but the rings cannot. That is the shape of IUT's Θ-link: it identifies the Θ-pilot \(\{q_v^{j^2}\}\) on one side with the \(q\)-pilot \(\{q_v\}\) on the other, multiplicatively, and the only things it lets through are abstract groups (the Galois groups \(G_v\)) and abstract monoids. If you want to say anything about the ring structure of \({}^\dagger R\) in terms of \({}^\ddagger R\), you need a procedure that starts from the shared group and rebuilds as much ring structure as possible on both sides by the same recipe. A procedure whose input is a bare group and whose output is a field is exactly what anabelian geometry, in its algorithmic form, provides.

Mochizuki puts the connection to abc in one sentence [Gateway, §4; Alien, §3.11 (iii)]: anabelian geometry is about reconstructing addition from multiplication (plus topology), and the abc inequality bounds the additive size of a number (its height) by its multiplicative size (its conductor). Both say that addition is dominated by multiplication. The rest of this guide is about how the reconstruction is actually done.

Otaku note: the premise of the series Two worlds, one shared object that passes between them, and the rule that you may not bring anything else across. It is the setup of every isekai and of every “switched bodies” film: in one famous one, the two leads cannot even keep each other's names, only the notes they leave. IUT's notes are Galois groups. And since the Japanese word 結び (musubi) means both “rice ball” and “a knot that ties two things together”, our mascot is an onigiri.

1What anabelian geometry is遠アーベル幾何とは

Every connected scheme \(X\) has an étale fundamental group \(\pi_1(X)\), a profinite group that classifies its finite étale covers the way the topological fundamental group classifies covering spaces. For a geometrically connected variety over a field \(k\) there is a fundamental exact sequence

\[ 1 \longrightarrow \Delta_X \longrightarrow \Pi_X \longrightarrow G_k \longrightarrow 1, \qquad \Pi_X=\pi_1(X),\;\; \Delta_X=\pi_1(X_{\overline k}),\;\; G_k=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline k/k). \]

\(\Delta_X\) is the geometric part (for a curve of genus \(g\) with \(r\) punctures it is the profinite completion of the topological fundamental group of a surface) and \(G_k\) is the arithmetic part. In his 1983 letter to Faltings, Grothendieck proposed that for “anabelian” varieties, whose fundamental groups are very far from abelian, the group \(\Pi_X\) together with its map to \(G_k\) should determine \(X\). The prototypes are:

  • Number fields (Neukirch, Uchida, 1969–1976): an isomorphism \(G_F\cong G_{F'}\) of absolute Galois groups of number fields comes from a unique isomorphism \(F'\cong F\).
  • Hyperbolic curves (Tamagawa 1997 for affine curves; Mochizuki 1996 and 1999 for all hyperbolic curves over sub-\(p\)-adic fields, i.e. subfields of finitely generated extensions of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\)): isomorphisms of \(\Pi_X\) over \(G_k\) come from isomorphisms of curves [pGC, LocAn].
  • Absolute results, where \(G_k\) is not given in advance and must itself be found inside \(\Pi_X\): [AbsAnab], [AbsTopI–III]. These are the ones IUT uses.

A curve is hyperbolic when \(2g-2+r>0\). That condition is what makes \(\Delta_X\) nonabelian and “rigid”: \(\mathbb{P}^1\) minus three points (a tripod) and an elliptic curve minus its origin are the two smallest examples, and both are the stars of this guide.

2Mono-anabelian versus bi-anabelian単遠アーベル と 双遠アーベル

There are two ways to say “the group determines the curve”. The classical way, which Mochizuki calls bi-anabelian, compares two objects: given \(X_1\), \(X_2\) and an isomorphism \(\Pi_{X_1}\cong\Pi_{X_2}\), it says the isomorphism comes from geometry. The mono-anabelian way is software: an algorithm that takes as input one abstract profinite group \(\Pi\), which “just happens” to be isomorphic to some \(\Pi_X\), and outputs field-like and scheme-like structures, functorially in \(\Pi\) [AbsTopIII, §I2, Rem 1.9.8]. The algorithm must be written “in language that only depends on the topological group structure”: no peeking at how \(\Pi\) was built.

Why IUT insists on mono: on the far side of a Θ-link (or a log-link) there is no reference model. The only thing you are handed is the group \(G_v\) or \(\Pi_v\) “as an abstract topological group”, and anything you build from it must be built the same way in both universes. [AbsTopIII, §3] makes this precise: the mono-anabelian algorithms are compatible with the log-Frobenius operation (Cor 3.6), and the bi-anabelian approach is not (Cor 3.7 and the remarks after it). Mochizuki also remarks that the Neukirch–Uchida theorem was historically proved bi-anabelianly, and that [AbsTopIII, Thm 1.9] gave the first independent proof with an explicit construction of the number field [AbsTopIII, Rem 1.9.5]. Hoshi later gave a mono-anabelian reconstruction of a number field from its absolute Galois group alone [Hoshi19], and wrote the standard introduction to the subject [Hoshi21].

Otaku note: spot the difference vs. the vending machine Bi-anabelian geometry is the “find the 7 differences” page of a weekend newspaper: two pictures side by side, and the theorem says there are zero differences. Mono-anabelian geometry is a Japanese vending machine: you put in one coin (a profinite group), press one button (the algorithm), and a hot can of corn soup (a field with \(+\) and \(\times\)) drops out. The machine has never seen your coin before and does not ask where you got it.

3Algorithm A: what \(G_k\) knows about a \(p\)-adic field局所体のガロア群

Start with the smallest input: \(G_k=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline k/k)\) for a finite extension \(k\) of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) (an MLF, mixed-characteristic local field). Local class field theory says \(G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong(k^\times)^\wedge\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mu_{q-1}\times\mu_{p^\infty}(k)\times\mathbb{Z}_p^{\,d}\), where \(q=p^f\) is the size of the residue field and \(d=[k:\mathbb{Q}_p]=ef\). That is the key to the following algorithm, which is [AbsAnab, Prop 1.2.1] read as software.

Algorithm A · input: an abstract profinite group \(G\cong G_k\)
  1. Compute \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\) and its rank over \(\mathbb{Z}_l\) for each prime \(l\). It is \(1\) for \(l\neq p\) and \(d+1\ge 2\) for \(l=p\). So \(p\) is the unique prime with rank at least \(2\), and \(d=[k:\mathbb{Q}_p]\) is that rank minus one.
  2. The prime-to-\(p\) torsion of \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\) has order \(q-1\). So \(q\), \(f=\log_p q\) and \(e=d/f\) are determined.
  3. An open subgroup \(H\) corresponds to an unramified extension exactly when \(e\) does not grow; the intersection of those is the inertia subgroup \(I_k\).
  4. The Frobenius element of \(G/I_k\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\) is the one that acts on the prime-to-\(p\) roots of unity of unramified \(H\) as \(x\mapsto x^{q}\).
  5. The images of \(\mathcal{O}_k^\times\), \(k^\times\) in \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\) follow, and taking the torsion of the abelianizations of all open \(H\), glued by the transfer (Verlagerung), gives a copy \(\mu_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}(G)\) of the roots of unity with its \(G\)-action: the cyclotomic character.
  6. Finally the Brauer-group residue map \(H^2(G,\mu_{\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}(G))\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\) of local class field theory is group-theoretic too [AbsAnab, Prop 1.2.1 (vii); AbsTopIII, Cor 1.10 (i)].

Look at the rows for \(\mathbb{Q}_3(\zeta_3)\) and \(\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt3)\) have the same \(p,d,q,f,e\), and Algorithm A only tells them apart through the \(p\)-power torsion. In general it cannot do more. There exist non-isomorphic \(p\)-adic fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups (Yamagata, Jarden–Ritter; see [NSW], the closing remark before Thm 12.2.7), and Mochizuki's 1997 paper records that he set out to prove the naive Grothendieck conjecture for \(p\)-adic fields and found it to be false [GCpLF, §0]. What is true is that \(G_k\) together with its higher ramification filtration determines \(k\) [GCpLF, Thm 4.2], a result proved with \(p\)-adic Hodge theory.

So \(G_k\) alone is mono-analytic: it sees the multiplicative monoid and its two dimensions, but not how addition ties them together. IUT calls the ring structure the arithmetic holomorphic structure, by analogy with a complex structure on a real surface [Alien, §2.7 (vii)]. To get it back you must couple \(G_k\) to geometry, which is the job of \(\Delta_X\). Remark 1.9.4 of [AbsTopIII] says this exactly: being “coupled with \(\Delta_X\)” via \(\Pi_X\) rigidifies both dimensions of \(G_k\).

There is one more useful version of the input. If you know not just \(G_k\) but the pair \(G_k\curvearrowright\mathcal{O}^{\rhd}_{\overline k}\) (a group acting on an abstract monoid, called an MLF-Galois pair), then the Brauer-group argument above recovers the identification of the monoid's roots of unity with \(\mu(G_k)\), and so \(\mathrm{Aut}(G_k\curvearrowright\mathcal{O}^{\rhd})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)\). With only the units, \(\mathrm{Aut}(G_k\curvearrowright\mathcal{O}^{\times})\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times\times\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)\): a whole \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times\) of ambiguity appears [Alien, Ex 2.12.1–2.12.2]. Remember this \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times\); it returns in §11.

Otaku note: identical twins \(G_k\) is the classic manga twin problem. The twins have the same height, the same birthday, the same grades (\(p\), \(d\), \(q\), \(f\), \(e\)) and only a beauty mark tells them apart (the \(p\)-power roots of unity), and sometimes not even that. The detective's solution is always the same: look at how each twin interacts with the rest of the world. For a \(p\)-adic field, “the rest of the world” is a hyperbolic curve over it.

4Algorithm B: Uchida's addition from multiplication内田の補題

Suppose you have somehow recovered the multiplicative group \(K_X^\times\) of the function field of a proper curve \(X\) over an algebraically closed field \(k\), together with, for each point \(x\), the valuation \(\mathrm{ord}_x\colon K_X^\times\to\mathbb{Z}\) and the subgroup \(U_x\) of functions with value \(1\) at \(x\). Then addition comes for free. This is “Uchida's lemma”, implicit in Uchida's 1977 work on function fields and stated as an algorithm in [AbsTopIII, Prop 1.3].

Algorithm B · input: \(\big(K_X^\times,\ \{\mathrm{ord}_x\},\ \{U_x\}\big)\)
  1. Constants: \(k^\times=\bigcap_x\ker(\mathrm{ord}_x)\). Evaluation: \(\ker(\mathrm{ord}_x)=U_x\times k^\times\), so the projection to \(k^\times\) is “the value at \(x\)”.
  2. The valuations let you talk about divisors, and so about \(\Gamma^\times(D)=\{f:\mathrm{Div}(f)+D\ge0\}\) and its dimension count \(l(D)\), all multiplicatively [Prop 1.1].
  3. Pick \(D\) with \(l(D)=2\) and three points \(x,y_1,y_2\) as in [Prop 1.2]. For \(\lambda\in k^\times\) let \(f_{\lambda,1}\) be the element of \(\Gamma^\times(D)\) with value \(\lambda\) at \(x\) and a zero at \(y_1\); define \(f_{\mu,2}\) likewise with a zero at \(y_2\).
  4. If \(\lambda\neq-\mu\), the sum \(f_{\lambda,1}+f_{\mu,2}\) is the unique \(g\in\Gamma^\times(D)\) with \(g(y_1)=f_{\mu,2}(y_1)\) and \(g(y_2)=f_{\lambda,1}(y_2)\). Then \(\lambda+\mu=g(x)\).
  5. Evaluating at all points gives addition on all of \(K_X^\times\cup\{0\}\).

Two lines on a graph do not look like deep mathematics, but notice what was not used: we never added two functions. We only used which functions vanish where, their values, and multiplication by constants. That is the whole philosophy of the subject in one picture. Mochizuki's summary of his reconstruction theorem is literally a sum of two terms [IUTchI, §I4]:

[AbsTopIII] §1 = Uchida's lemma + Belyi cuspidalization

and over finite fields the analogous slogan is “Uchida's lemma + the Lefschetz trace formula for Frobenius” [IUTchI, §I4]. The next three sections supply the inputs to Uchida's lemma from \(\Pi_X\) alone: the cyclotome (§5), the Kummer classes (§5) and the points (§6–7).

Otaku note: a sudoku that outputs arithmetic The puzzle magazine that gave sudoku its name has a rule that every grid must have a unique solution. Uchida's lemma is that rule applied to a linear system: two given values pin down one function, and its third value is the answer. You can do \(3+5\) this way, and the build script does. It is the least efficient calculator in Japan, which is a competitive category.

5Algorithm C: cyclotomes and Kummer classes円分物とクンマー類

Synchronizing cyclotomes

Algorithm B needs functions, and to get functions out of a group you need Kummer theory, and Kummer theory needs coefficients: a copy of \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)=\varprojlim\mu_N\). A group gives you several candidates, called cyclotomes, and the first job is to identify them with each other canonically. For a proper curve \(X\) with a point \(x\) removed, [AbsTopIII, Prop 1.4] gives a group-theoretic isomorphism between

  • the inertia group \(I_x\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)\) of the puncture (the loop around the hole), and
  • \(M_X=\mathrm{Hom}(H^2(\Delta_X,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}),\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\), the dual of the “fundamental class” of the closed surface,

using the maximal cuspidally central extension \(1\to I_x\to\Delta^{\text{c-cn}}_{X\setminus x}\to\Delta_X\to 1\): its extension class is an element of \(\mathrm{Hom}(M_X,I_x)\), and it is the isomorphism. Topologically: a small loop around the puncture is homologous to the boundary of the whole surface, so the “local” and “global” orientation are the same thing.

Kummer classes

Given a cyclotome, every invertible function \(f\) on an open \(U\subseteq X\) has a Kummer class \(\kappa_U(f)\in H^1(\Pi_U,M_X)\): the class of the torsor of all \(N\)-th roots of \(f\), for all \(N\). The base field must be Kummer-faithful (no nonzero element of any semi-abelian variety is infinitely divisible [AbsTopIII, Def 1.5]); every sub-\(p\)-adic field is [Rem 1.5.4]. Then [Prop 1.6] says:

  1. \(\kappa_U\) is injective, so functions are faithfully recorded as cohomology classes;
  2. restricting to the inertia groups \(I_x\) of the punctures gives an exact sequence \(1\to(k^\times)^\wedge\to H^1(\Pi_U,M_X)\to\bigoplus_{x\in S}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\), where the map to the sum reads off the order of zero or pole at each puncture;
  3. the image of the functions is cut out by the condition that this divisor be principal, and principality is tested group-theoretically with the sections \(G_k\to\Pi_{J}\) of the Jacobian.

Then [Prop 1.8] separates nonconstant functions from constants: a class is a nonconstant number-field function when some multiple of it vanishes at one point and not at another (values are computed by restricting to the decomposition group of the point, \(\eta|_x=s_x^*(\eta)\in H^1(G_{k_x},M_X)\)), and a constant is a value of such a function at a point.

Otaku note: the tuning fork and the transformation belt Before a school brass band plays, everyone tunes to one note. The cyclotome is that note: the inertia loop, the fundamental class of the surface and the roots of unity in the field all have to agree on pitch before anything else can be played. The Kummer map is the transformation belt of a long-running masked-rider franchise: insert a function, and it comes out as a cohomology class with the same personality and a much better costume. Cyclotomic rigidity is reading the belt's instructions so that you do not transform into \(f^{-1}\) by mistake.

6Algorithm D: Belyi cuspidalizationベールイ・カスプ化

Uchida's lemma needs points: the valuations \(\mathrm{ord}_x\) and the evaluations at \(x\). In the group world a point \(x\) shows up as its decomposition group \(D_x\subseteq\Pi_X\), but nothing in the group structure of \(\Pi_X\) singles that subgroup out (it is the image of a section of \(\Pi_X\twoheadrightarrow G_k\), and there are many sections). The trick is to turn points into cusps, because cusps are visible: their inertia groups can be found group-theoretically [AbsTopI, Lemma 4.5; IUTchI, Rem 1.2.2]. “Cuspidalization” means constructing \(\Pi_{X\setminus S}\twoheadrightarrow\Pi_X\) from \(\Pi_X\) alone. The tool is Belyi's theorem (1979): a curve over \(\mathbb{C}\) is defined over a number field exactly when it admits a map to \(\mathbb{P}^1\) branched over at most three points.

The idea [AbsTopII, Ex 3.6, Cor 3.7]: take the point \(x\) you want and a Belyi map \(\beta\colon W\to P\), where \(P=\mathbb{P}^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}\), \(W\subseteq U\subseteq V\) are open, and \(x\) is one of the points \(\beta\) sends to \(\{0,1,\infty\}\). Over \(P\) the map is finite étale, so \(\Pi_W\) is an open subgroup of \(\Pi_P\), which you can find by group theory. Then \(W\subseteq U\setminus\{x\}\) is an open immersion, so \(\Pi_W\twoheadrightarrow\Pi_{U\setminus\{x\}}\) is a quotient by inertia groups of cusps, which you can also find. A chain of “cover, then fill in cusps” operations takes you from \(\Pi_P\) to \(\Pi_{U\setminus\{x\}}\).

Algorithm D · [AbsTopII] Cor 3.7 · input: \(1\to\Delta\to\Pi\to G\to1\), \(X\) of strictly Belyi type
  1. (a) Find a normal open \(\Pi_V\subseteq\Pi\) and a chain of type \(\subset,\subset,\bullet,\dots,\bullet,\subset,\bullet,\dots,\bullet,\supset\) (covers \(\subset\), de-cuspidalizations \(\bullet\)) ending in a terminal isomorphism with the trivial chain. It yields \(\Pi_U\twoheadrightarrow\Pi_V\) for \(U=V\times_X U_X\).
  2. (b) Descend to \(\Pi_{U_X}\twoheadrightarrow\Pi\) using the unique lift of the outer action of \(\Pi/\Pi_V\).
  3. (c) The decomposition groups of the points of \(X\setminus U_X\) are the images of the cuspidal decomposition groups of \(\Pi_{U_X}\).

“Strictly Belyi type” means defined over a number field and isogenous to a hyperbolic curve of genus 0 [AbsTopII, Def 3.5]; every finite étale cover of a once-punctured elliptic curve over a number field qualifies [Alien, Ex 2.12.3 (i)]. The method reaches points defined over number fields; over a \(p\)-adic field all closed points follow by \(p\)-adic approximation [AbsTopII, Rem 3.7.2].

Belyi's own algorithm makes every algebraic point reachable: first push the point to a rational one by repeatedly composing with minimal polynomials, then use the map \(t\mapsto\frac{(m+n)^{m+n}}{m^m n^n}t^m(1-t)^n\), which is ramified only over \(0,1,\infty\) and sends \(\frac{m}{m+n}\mapsto1\). For \(\frac25\) the script checks that \(\beta(\frac25)=1\), that \(\frac25\) is a double root of \(\beta=1\), that the other three roots are \({{belyiR0}}\) and \({{belyiRe}}\pm{{belyiIm}}\,i\), and that Schreier's formula gives \({{belyiDeg}}\cdot1+1={{belyiPunct}}-1\), as it must for \({{belyiPunct}}\) punctures. Another example: \(4(t-1)/t^2\) does the same job for the point \(2\).

Otaku note: origami and the rolling ball A Belyi map is origami for Riemann surfaces: you fold the whole sphere onto itself until the point you care about lands on one of three corners, and the creases (the branch points) all sit on the corners too. There is also a well-loved PlayStation game in which a tiny prince rolls a sticky ball over everything until the whole world is stuck to it. Belyi's algorithm is that ball: point after point gets rolled into \(\{0,1,\infty\}\), and none of them ever falls off. The King of All Cosmos would approve of the degree.

7Algorithm E: elliptic cuspidalization楕円カスプ化

For a once-punctured elliptic curve \(X=E\setminus\{O\}\) there is a second, more elementary way to turn points into cusps, which works for torsion points and also in pro-\(\Sigma\) settings where Belyi maps are unavailable [AbsTopII, Ex 3.2, Cor 3.3]. Multiplication by \(N\) on \(E\) restricts to a finite étale map

\[ [N]\colon\; U=E\setminus E[N]\;\longrightarrow\; E\setminus\{O\}=X \qquad\text{of degree }N^2. \]

So \(\Pi_U\) is an open subgroup of \(\Pi_X\) of index \(N^2\), visible to group theory. But \(U\) is also an open subscheme of \(X\), so \(\Pi_U\twoheadrightarrow\Pi_X\) by filling in the \(N^2-1\) nonzero torsion points. The same group plays both roles; following the chain “cover by \([N]\), then de-cuspidalize \(N^2-1\) times” recovers the cuspidalization \(\Pi_U\twoheadrightarrow\Pi_X\) and with it the decomposition groups of the \(N\)-torsion points. The general statement handles any “elliptically admissible” orbicurve: one whose \(k\)-core is semi-elliptic, i.e. a quotient of a once-punctured elliptic curve by \(\pm1\) [AbsTopII, Def 3.1].

In IUT this is how the evaluation points are reached: the theta function is evaluated at \(\mu_-\)-translates of cusps, which are torsion points, and their decomposition groups are reconstructed by elliptic cuspidalization [Alien, §3.4 (iii)]. It is also behind [IUTchI, Cor 1.2], which reconstructs \(\Pi_{\underline X}\), \(\Pi_{\vec C}\), \(\Pi_{\underline C}\) and the cusps \(0,\epsilon',\epsilon''\) from \(\Pi_{\vec X}\), and recovers the prime \(l\) itself from an index count.

8The full algorithm: [AbsTopIII] Theorem 1.9復元定理

Now everything plugs together. Let \(X\) be a hyperbolic orbicurve of strictly Belyi type over a sub-\(p\)-adic field \(k\) (a number field or a \(p\)-adic field, say). [AbsTopIII, Thm 1.9] gives a functorial group-theoretic algorithm that reconstructs, from the extension \(1\to\Delta_X\to\Pi_X\to G_k\to1\) alone, the “NF-portion of the function field”: the field \(\overline k_{\mathrm{NF}}\) of algebraic numbers in \(\overline k\) and the function fields of the covers of \(X\) over it, with their field structures. When \(k\) is a number field or an MLF the extension can be replaced by the single group \(\Pi_X\) [Rem 1.9.2; AbsTopI, Thm 2.6 (v), (vi)].

Three remarks from the paper are worth keeping in your pocket.

  • Combinatorialization [Rem 1.9.7]: the algorithm is “a complete combinatorialization, independent of the base field, of the orbicurve”, in the same spirit as replacing a scheme's covers by an abstract Galois category, or a log-regular scheme's local rings by monoids.
  • An elementary version [Thm 1.11]: if you drop Belyi cuspidalization and only want the function field, there is a much simpler algorithm valid over any Kummer-faithful field. Mochizuki hopes it can be taught to advanced undergraduates.
  • MLF bonus [Cor 1.10]: over a \(p\)-adic field, \(\Pi_X\) also gives back \(\overline k\), \(k\) and the ring structure of \(\overline k\), not only its algebraic-number part. This is the local result IUT uses at each prime.
Otaku note: the culinary battle In a famous cooking show the chairman unveils a single ingredient and the chefs must build a whole menu from it in an hour. Theorem 1.9 is that show with \(\Pi_X\) under the cloche. Course one, the Galois group (Algorithm A); course two, a cyclotome reduction (§5); the main course, Belyi-folded points with a Kummer glaze (§6); dessert, Uchida's addition. The judges taste it in another universe and it is the same dish. Allez cuisine!

9The archimedean side: Aut-holomorphic spacesアルキメデス的側面

A number field also has complex places, and there is no profinite Galois group there. [AbsTopIII, §2] supplies the archimedean counterpart: an Aut-holomorphic space is a topological surface together with, for every connected open \(U\), the subgroup \(\mathrm{Aut}^{\mathrm{hol}}(U)\subseteq\mathrm{Homeo}(U)\) of holomorphic automorphisms [Def 2.1]. The key fact is that the holomorphic-or-antiholomorphic automorphisms of the unit disc form a commensurably terminal subgroup of its homeomorphisms [Prop 2.2], so this weak-looking data pins down the complex structure up to complex conjugation. A refinement of the earlier “parallelograms, rectangles, squares” method then recovers the local linear holomorphic structure [Prop 2.5, 2.6], and elliptic cuspidalization on the Riemann surface ties it to the Galois-theoretic theory of §1 [Cor 2.7–2.9].

Otaku note: the ghost and the shell In a famous cyberpunk manga, a cyborg's “ghost” can move between bodies, and the question is what survives the move. Here the shell is the topological surface, which anyone can copy, and the ghost is the holomorphic structure. The Aut-holomorphic structure is a way of carrying the ghost in a form that the shell can hold: not the complex coordinate itself, but the list of motions that respect it.

10Étale-like, Frobenius-like, and mono-anabelian transportエタール的とフロベニウス的

The prototype in positive characteristic

Mochizuki calls the following example “the fundamental model, or prototype, for a quite substantial portion of inter-universal Teichmüller theory” [Alien, Ex 2.6.1]. Let \(K\) be the function field of a curve over a finite field of characteristic \(p\). The Frobenius \(\Phi\colon f\mapsto f^p\) has degree \(p\), so it is far from an isomorphism, yet it induces an isomorphism \(\Psi\colon G_K\xrightarrow{\sim}G_K\) of Galois groups, because it is purely inseparable. There are two ways to rebuild \(K\) from \(G_K\): by class field theory (which uses the ring structure heavily) and by Kummer theory (which uses only the multiplicative monoid and one cyclotomic rigidity isomorphism \(\lambda\)). Either way, the square below cannot commute.

The vocabulary

From this example IUT takes its terms [Alien, §2.7]. A link is a wall between two “mutually alien” copies of scheme theory (the Θ-link and the log-link). Frobenius-like objects are the ones used to set up the link; they live on one side only, carry “positive mass” (degrees, heights) and are changed by the link, like \(K\) under \(\Phi\). Étale-like objects are groups such as \(G_K\), \(\Pi_X\), and everything built from them by an algorithm; they are “zero mass” containers, mapped isomorphically across the link, though only by an unknown isomorphism. An object that maps isomorphically across a link is coric.

Mono-anabelian transport computes what the link does to positive-mass data in three moves: (1) Kummer theory passes from Frobenius-like to étale-like objects (Kummer-detachment); (2) the anabelian algorithm, being the same on both sides, carries the étale-like objects across (étale-transport); (3) inverse Kummer theory re-attaches Frobenius-like objects on the far side. Each move has its own indeterminacy: Kummer-detachment indeterminacies (mostly about cyclotomic rigidity) and étale-transport indeterminacies (the étale-like objects are only known up to isomorphism) [IUTchIII, Rem 1.5.4].

The same example also explains the two words arithmetic holomorphic and mono-analytic [Alien, §2.7 (vii)]. The class-field-theory reconstruction depends on the whole ring; the Kummer reconstruction depends only on the multiplicative monoid plus \(\lambda\). Kummer theory “isolates the dependence on the ring structure in the compact form of the cyclotomic rigidity isomorphism”. That is why the next section is entirely about \(\lambda\).

Otaku note: ekiben on the bullet train A station bento (ekiben) is a box designed to survive a high-speed journey. The box is étale-like: rigid, standard, the same in Tokyo and in Hakata, weighing nothing that matters. The food is Frobenius-like: it is what you actually care about, and it is different in every region. Mono-anabelian transport ships the boxes and re-fills them on arrival, and the indeterminacies are the passengers' complaints that the karaage moved around in transit. They are allowed to complain only up to a bounded amount.

11Cyclotomic rigidity, three ways円分剛性の三つの方法

Everything Frobenius-like gets into the étale-like world through Kummer theory, and Kummer theory is only as good as its cyclotomic rigidity isomorphism: the identification of the roots of unity of the monoid with the cyclotome of the group. IUT needs this identification to be multiradial: it must survive arbitrary automorphisms of the coric data on the other side of the Θ-link. That data contains \(G_v\curvearrowright\mathcal{O}^{\times\mu}\), and \(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times\) acts on it (raise units to the power \(\lambda\)); this action moves the roots of unity of the monoid but not the cyclotome of the group. So any rigidity that depends on the unit group is ruined by the Θ-link, and IUT must decouple the value-group data from the units [Alien, §3.4 (i)]. It uses three methods, summarized in [Alien, Fig 3.10]:

The global method: \(\kappa\)-coric functions

The global realified Frobenioids that cross the Θ-link are, in essence, arithmetic line bundles on copies of the number field \(F_{\mathrm{mod}}\). Elements of \(F_{\mathrm{mod}}\) are produced as values of functions at special points, exactly as in [AbsTopIII, §1] [Alien, §3.4 (ii)]. The functions used are the \(\kappa\)-coric rational functions on the coarse curve \(|C_{F_{\mathrm{mod}}}|\cong\mathbb{A}^1\): those that take root-of-unity values at the “strictly critical” points (the nonzero 2-torsion points) [IUTchI, Rem 3.1.7]. That condition can be checked on the étale-like side by restricting Kummer classes to decomposition groups, and it is what decouples them from the local units. Their rigidity comes from the elementary fact \(\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\cap\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times=\{1\}\): if \(\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times\) sends the Kummer class of every function to the class of a function, then looking at orders of zeros (positive integers) forces \(\lambda=\pm1\) [Alien, Ex 2.13.1 (ii)]. The leftover \(\pm1\) is harmless because \(F_{\mathrm{mod}}^\times\) is closed under inversion. The price is that the argument only works for the full profinite group, not for its finite quotients; that does not matter globally [Alien, §3.4 (v)].

The local method: mono-theta environments

At a bad prime \(v\), \(X_v\) is a once-punctured Tate curve and its tempered cover \(Y_v\) has special fibre an infinite chain of projective lines (Figure 12). The theta function \(\ddot\Theta_v\) has a simple zero at every cusp, which allows a “mod \(N\)” version of the \(\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\cap\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times\) argument with no \(\pm1\) and compatible with the topology. But its poles are not symmetric under the \(\mathbb{Z}\)-action, so the naive version fails discrete rigidity. The fix is to package the data as a triple (the \(\mathbb{G}_m\)-torsor of the line bundle, its automorphisms by symmetries and constants, the theta section): the mono-theta environment [EtTh, Def 2.13]. It is invariant under the \(l\cdot\mathbb{Z}\)-symmetries, and commutators of its automorphisms recover cyclotomic rigidity; all of this is “essentially a formal consequence of the quadratic structure of the commutators of the theta groups” [Alien, §3.4 (iv)]. Mono-theta environments have étale-like versions (from \(\Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_v\)) and Frobenius-like versions (from a tempered Frobenioid), which is what lets the theta values travel by mono-anabelian transport [IUTchII, Prop 1.2, Cor 1.10–1.12].

Otaku note: three keys to one door A classic adventure series on a certain handheld always hides the last dungeon behind a door with three locks. The first key (class field theory) opens it, but it only works from inside the room, which is useless if you are in another universe. The second key (mono-theta) works from anywhere and fits the lock exactly, but only at the bad primes. The third key (\(\kappa\)-coric functions) works globally, but it only turns if you hold the entire profinite group at once, never a finite piece of it. IUT carries all three, and its inventory screen is Fig 3.10.

12The log-link and log-shells対数リンクと対数殻

The \(p\)-adic logarithm \(\log_k\colon\mathcal{O}_k^\times\to k\) is not a ring map (it turns \(\times\) into \(+\)), but it is \(G_k\)-equivariant. That makes it a link in the sense of §10, with \(\Pi_X\) coric [AbsTopIII, §I1; Alien, Ex 2.12.3]: in the chain \(\cdots\xrightarrow{\log}\mathcal{O}_k\xrightarrow{\log}\mathcal{O}_k\xrightarrow{\log}\cdots\), each copy of \(\mathcal{O}_k\) belongs to a different universe, but the same \(\Pi_X\) sits over all of them, and Corollary 1.10 rebuilds the same field structure from each. The diagram of Kummer maps and logs does not commute as maps of elements, but it does commute “as regions”. Put

\[ \mathcal{I}=(2p)^{-1}\cdot\log_k(\mathcal{O}_k^\times)\subseteq k, \qquad \mathcal{O}_k\subseteq\mathcal{I}\supseteq\log_k(\mathcal{O}_k^\times). \]

Every composite of arrows, through any number of log-iterates, lands inside the étale-like log-shell \(\mathcal{I}(\Pi_X)\). This is upper semi-commutativity, and together with the fact that log-volumes are compatible with \(\log\) it is all IUT needs from the log-link [Alien, Ex 2.12.3 (iv); IUTchIII, Prop 1.2, Rem 1.2.2]. The log-shell comes in four flavours: holomorphic or mono-analytic, Frobenius-like or étale-like; the mono-analytic ones are built from \(\mathcal{O}^{\times\mu}\) alone, since \(\mathcal{O}^{\times\mu}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\).

Otaku note: the futon and the time loop The log-link is a time-loop anime: every episode the world resets, and the protagonist keeps only what fits in a small container. The log-shell is that container, and its whole job is to be big enough that nothing from any previous loop sticks out. For an unramified field and an odd prime it fits exactly, like a futon folded into its closet. Ramification is when the futon is a little too big and the closet door will not quite shut, which is what the \(\log(\mathfrak{d})\) terms in [IUTchIV] keep track of.

Honest notes

What the toolkit does. The C toolkit and the web app model IUT's data types and processes and keep their bookkeeping (labels, indices, log-volumes, indeterminacy counts). They do not compute with profinite groups, and no finite program can run Theorem 1.9 on an actual \(\Pi_X\). The numbers in this guide come either from that bookkeeping or from the small worked examples in build.js, which are checked when the PDF is built.

What is and is not contested. The anabelian papers surveyed here ([AbsAnab], [AbsTopI–III], [EtTh]) were published before and independently of IUT. The disagreement that followed the March 2018 discussions between Scholze, Stix and Mochizuki concerns the proof of [IUTchIII] Corollary 3.12, in particular how the identifications across the Θ-link may be made; Mochizuki's position is set out in [EssLgc]. This guide describes the reconstruction machinery that both sides of that discussion take as input, and adjudicates nothing.

What we simplified. We wrote “the” fundamental group where basepoints and outer isomorphisms matter; we ignored orbicurve technicalities, the tempered versions at bad primes [AbsTopIII, Rem 1.9.1], and the minor corrections to [AbsAnab, Lemma 1.3.9] and [AbsTopI, Lemma 4.5] recorded in [IUTchI, Rem 1.2.2].
