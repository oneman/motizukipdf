1The plot in one breath一息であらすじ

Rebuild the theta values of one universe out of the parts of it that survive the trip into another universe, accept a little blur, and then weigh the result with a scale that both universes trust.

That sentence is the multiradial algorithm and its corollary. The rest of this guide unpacks it. First, why anyone would want to do this.

1.1The N-th power trick

Take a nonzero integer \(x\) and measure its size by its height \(h(x) = \log|x|\). Raising to the \(N\)-th power multiplies the height by \(N\):

\[ h(x^N) \;=\; N\cdot h(x). \]

Now suppose someone hands you a machine that, given only some data that \(x\) and \(x^N\) have in common, rebuilds the size of \(x^N\) in the coordinates of \(x\), and is wrong by at most \(\varepsilon\). Then \(N\,h(x) \le h(x) + \varepsilon\), so

\[ h(x) \;\le\; \frac{\varepsilon}{N-1}. \]

A machine that “cannot tell \(x\) from \(x^N\) up to a small error” bounds the height. This is the whole strategy of inter-universal Teichmüller theory in one line, and it is how Mochizuki himself summarizes it in his April 2026 progress report on formalizing the theory in Lean [Form26, §3–§4]: the essential content of Corollary 3.12 is “that the height of the elliptic curve under consideration is equal to \(N\) times the height of the elliptic curve (where \(N\) is a large positive number), up to a small discrepancy (arising from (Ind1,2,3) + hull), thus implying a bound on the height.”

Of course no such machine exists for integers inside ordinary arithmetic: \(x \mapsto x^N\) is not a ring homomorphism of \(\mathbb{Z}\), and nothing about \(\mathbb{Z}\) “forgets” the difference between \(x\) and \(x^N\). The theory's answer is to put \(x\) and \(x^N\) in two different copies of arithmetic, glue the copies along the part they can share, and then build the machine. The gluing is the Θ-link. The machine is the multiradial algorithm.

1.2What plays the role of \(x\) and \(x^N\)

In IUT the role of \(x\) is played by the q-pilot: the \(2l\)-th roots \(\underline{q}_v\) of the Tate parameters of the elliptic curve at its bad places \(v\). The role of \(x^N\) is played by the Θ-pilot: the collection of theta values

\[ \Bigl\{\, \underline{q}_v^{\,j^2} \Bigr\}_{j = 1, \dots, l^*}, \qquad l^* = \tfrac{l-1}{2}, \]

one for each nonzero label \(j\) up to sign. So there is not one exponent \(N\) but a whole row of them, \(1, 4, 9, \dots, (l^*)^2\), and their average is

\[ \frac{1}{l^*}\sum_{j=1}^{l^*} j^2 \;=\; \frac{l(l+1)}{12} \;=\; {{dilation}} \quad (l = {{l}}). \]

Both pilots have a log-volume, which is (minus) their arithmetic degree. For our running example \(1 + 8 = 9\) the q-pilot has log-volume \(-|\log \underline{q}| = {{qpilot}}\) and the Θ-pilot has naive log-volume \({{thetapilot}} = -{{dilation}}\cdot|\log\underline{q}|\). The Θ-link declares these two objects to be “the same” (as objects of an abstract monoid, not of a ring), and the multiradial algorithm then shows that the declaration costs almost nothing.

Otaku corner · 君の名は。

The cleanest anime analogy for the Θ-link is the body-swap film Your Name (Kimi no Na wa, 2016). Two teenagers live in two towns, three years apart, and wake up in each other's bodies. What they share is the body (the prime-strip); what they do not share is the town, the year, the friends, the whole “ring structure” of daily life. Neither can phone the other, because their worlds do not even agree on the date. So they leave notes on the phone, written so that they make sense whoever reads them. Those notes are the multiradial representation: messages about one world, written in terms that are valid in both (“simultaneous holomorphic expressibility”, §6.6). And the film's catastrophe is only avoided because of what both sides can agree on: a comet with a measurable size, much like the log-volume that Corollary 3.12 reads off at the end.
