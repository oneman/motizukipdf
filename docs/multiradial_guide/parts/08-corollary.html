Corollary 3.12 · Log-volume estimates for Θ-pilot objects

Let \(-|\log(\underline{\underline{\Theta}})| \in \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\) be the procession-normalized mono-analytic log-volume of the holomorphic hull of the union of the possible images of a Θ-pilot object in the multiradial representation of Theorem 3.11, subject to (Ind1), (Ind2), (Ind3). Let \(-|\log(\underline{q})| \in \mathbb{R}\) be the procession-normalized log-volume of the image of a q-pilot object, not subject to the indeterminacies. Then \(-|\log(\underline{\underline{\Theta}})| \in \mathbb{R}\) and

\[ -|\log(\underline{\underline{\Theta}})| \;\ge\; -|\log(\underline{q})|, \]

i.e. \(C_\Theta \ge -1\) for any real \(C_\Theta\) with \(-|\log(\underline{\underline{\Theta}})| \le C_\Theta\cdot|\log(\underline{q})|\).

Two asymmetries in the statement are the whole point. The Θ-side is blurred and hulled; the q-side is sharp. And the inequality goes the “surprising” way: naively the Θ-pilot is the bigger object (its log-volume is more negative, \({{thetapilot}}\) against \({{qpilot}}\) for \(1+8=9\)), yet the corollary says the blurred Θ-pilot cannot be smaller than the q-pilot. The only way both can be true is if the blur is at least as big as the gap. That is the height bound in disguise (§9).

8.1The proof, in Mochizuki's twelve steps

The proof begins with a free move: since \(|\log\underline{q}| > 0\), one may assume \(-|\log\Theta| < 0\), because otherwise \(-|\log\Theta| \ge 0 > -|\log\underline{q}|\) and there is nothing to prove. Then it reviews “precisely what is achieved” by the theory, in steps:

(ii)Units suffer \(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{F}^{\times\mu}}\) (that is, (Ind1), (Ind2)); the cyclotomes behind the value-group portion (mono-theta environments, κ-coric functions) are insulated from them.

8.2Substeps (xi-a)–(xi-g): two ways to compute one number

  1. (xi-a) Think of the q-pilot at \((1,0)\) in terms of the holomorphic log-shells there. The Θ-link is a gluing that makes the Θ-pilot at \((0,0)\) correspond to the q-pilot at \((1,0)\).
  2. (xi-b) The multiradial algorithm produces a collection of possible output data in \({}^{0,\circ}\mathcal{U} \xrightarrow{\sim} {}^{1,\circ}\mathcal{U}\), the arrow coming from the étale-picture symmetry. For the rest of the argument, forget the theta functions (HIS) and keep only IPL and SHE.
  3. (xi-c) So: the output possibilities are linked to the q-pilot's representation on \({}^{1,\circ}\mathcal{U}^{\mathbb{Q}}\) and can be expressed entirely in the ring structure of column 1. Pass to the holomorphic hull: the possibilities become localizations of arithmetic vector bundles over the rings \(\mathcal{O}_{K_v}\) labelled \((1,\circ)\).
  4. (xi-d) Those bundles have rank \(>1\) and the q-pilot is a line bundle, so take (a power of) the determinant and then the log-volume. Two comparable objects result: \(\mathbb{R}_{\le -|\log\Theta|}\) and \(-|\log\underline{q}| \in \mathbb{R}\). The log-Kummer correspondence in column 1 corrects the vertical shift between the units of \({}^{1,0}\mathcal{F}^{\times\mu}\) and the log-shells built from them.
  5. (xi-e) Expressibility in column 1's ring structure means: valid even under the condition that the input prime-strip's pilot log-volume equals the fixed value \(-|\log\underline{q}|\).
  6. (xi-f) Hence the construction of \(\mathbb{R}_{\le -|\log\Theta|}\) “constitutes, in particular, a construction […] of the pilot-object log-volume of the input data”, namely \(-|\log\underline{q}|\). So \(-|\log\underline{q}| \in \mathbb{R}_{\le-|\log\Theta|}\), and the inequality “follows formally”.
  7. (xi-g) In summary: two tautologically equivalent ways to compute the log-volume of the q-pilot at \((1,0)\) (Figure 8.1).
The punchline in one line

The set of log-volumes the algorithm can output, \(\mathbb{R}_{\le -|\log\Theta|}\), was constructed by an algorithm that, run on the q-pilot's own input, is a way of computing the q-pilot's log-volume. So the q-pilot's log-volume is one of the outputs: \(-|\log\underline{q}| \le -|\log\Theta|\).

8.3The hull, the determinant and the closed loop (Stp4–Stp8)

In [EssLgc] §3.10 the same substeps appear as five moves, each of which “does not affect the logical ‘∧/∨’ structure” or only adds possibilities:

  • (Stp4) Hull. Pass from the possible regions to the holomorphic hull in column 1: a “simple, straightforward” enlargement of the set of possibilities.
  • (Stp5) Hull-approximants. Replace each region by one with the same global log-volume that corresponds to an arithmetic vector bundle.
  • (Stp6) Determinant. Pass to a suitable rational tensor power of the determinant line bundle.
  • (Stp7) Log-shift adjustment. The line bundles of (Stp6) live in the ring structure at \((1,1)\), one step above the Θ-link's codomain. Running (Stp1)–(Stp6) at every \((1,m)\) gives arrows from \((1,m)\) to \((1,m+1)\): “a sort of endomorphism of the 1-column”, a closed loop. Because log-volumes are compatible with log-links, the shift costs nothing.
  • (Stp8) Log-volumes. The closed loop means the logical AND relation kept through (Stp1)–(Stp7) becomes an AND relation between the q-pilot's log-volume and the set of possible output log-volumes “within the same container, i.e., some copy of the real numbers”. The inequality follows formally.

8.4The 2026 reorganization: 3.11 ⇒ 3.11.5 ⇒ 3.12

For the Lean formalization Mochizuki regroups the argument [Form26, §4]. A diagram of species (types of objects) and mutations (functorial algorithms) has four commuting triangles. The first two express the multiradial algorithm, with input a BPS (basic prime-strip, i.e. F×μ-prime-strip), as a descent (dsc): it only needs a BPS glued to the vertically coric étale Hodge theater (etHT). The third composes it with “hull+det” to get (HDD). The fourth restricts (HDD) along the SHE arrow to the case where the input is the q-pilot. The never-stated “Theorem 3.11.5 (= 3.11 + Rmk. 3.9.5)” is the statement after the third triangle, and “3.11.5 ⇒ 3.12” is the simultaneous comparison of Θ-pilot and q-pilot in one ring structure. Skeletal Lean code exists for this last step; work on the second and third triangles (the APT property) is under way at RIMS.

8.5Three “why” questions the proof answers

Why only one Θ-link? Because the lattice does not commute. Relating units through one Θ-link and value groups through another would need a commuting square (Step (iii)).

Why global Frobenioids? The Θ-link only preserves isomorphism classes of objects. Locally, a region and its \(p^N\)-multiple are related by endomorphisms that are not automorphisms, so a purely local argument could not even tell them apart. Global realified Frobenioids have no such endomorphisms (Step (xii)).

Why not use \(\Theta^N\) and get a sharper bound? Because the argument rests on the first power of the étale theta function: its mono-theta cyclotomic rigidity has no evident analogue for \(N\)-th tensor powers, whose log-volume “must always be computed as the result of multiplying the log-volume of the original Θ-pilot object by \(N\)”. Mochizuki adds that the sharper inequalities this would give “are known to be false” (Step (xi-h), [IUTchIV] Rem. 2.3.2 (ii)). On the other hand, replacing the q-pilot by \(\underline{q}^\lambda\) on the codomain side is harmless and gives \(C_\Theta \ge -\lambda\) (Rem. 3.12.1 (ii)).

Otaku corner · 風呂敷

The holomorphic hull is a furoshiki. You have a pile of oddly shaped objects (the possible regions); to carry them on the train you wrap them in one square cloth, tied at the corners. The cloth is bigger than the objects and its size is easy to state (“a 70 cm furoshiki”), which is the point. Corollary 3.12 is the statement that the q-pilot fits in the cloth. A sceptic might ask whether the cloth is so big that anything would fit; §9 shows how big it is for real curves, and §12 is where people argue about it.
