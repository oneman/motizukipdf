The Multiradial Algorithm

A field guide to [IUTchIII] Theorem 3.11 and Corollary 3.12: what goes in, what comes out, what gets blurred on the way, and why the blur turns into an inequality. With analogies, figures, real numbers, and an irresponsible amount of anime. （多輻的アルゴリズム完全攻略ガイド）

Running example: initial Θ-data for \(1 + 8 = 9\) with \(l = {{l}}\) · three more worked examples · every number comes from the toolkit in src/ · November 2026
Abstract   The multiradial algorithm is the engine of inter-universal Teichmüller theory. It takes a small piece of data that two incompatible “universes” of ring theory share, and from it rebuilds, in the language of one universe, the theta values that were built in the other, up to three controlled blurs called (Ind1), (Ind2) and (Ind3). Corollary 3.12 then measures the blurred picture with a ruler both universes agree on, the log-volume, and reads off an inequality. This guide walks through the algorithm step by step, in Mochizuki's own order (Theorem 3.11, the proof of Corollary 3.12, the steps (Stp1)–(Stp8) of [EssLgc] §3.10), and runs every step on four sets of initial Θ-data that the toolkit in this repository builds exactly. It collects the analogies Mochizuki himself uses (parallel transport, crystals, the Gaussian integral, a CPU and a network cable) and adds a few of our own, most of them from Japanese pop culture. It ends with an honest account of where the debate about Corollary 3.12 stands in November 2026, including the July 2026 interim report of the LANA formalization project.
Content notice Jokes are text only: no character art, logos or screenshots. Where we say what the theory claims, we cite the papers in pdf/. Where people disagree about whether the claim is proved, we report each side in its own words and adjudicate nothing (§12). Numbers marked “toolkit” are produced by the C library in src/ when this PDF is built; for curves this small they illustrate the shape of the computation, not a sharp bound (§9.4). 審判ではありません。

How to read this guide

Three speeds. Express (ten minutes): read §1, look at Figure 6.1 and Figure 8.1, read the boxed “key” statements. Local (an evening): read §1–§9 in order and skip the green paper boxes. Kakueki-teisha 各駅停車 (stops at every station): read everything and keep [IUTchIII] open at pages 153–186. Boxes are colour-coded: pink for jokes, purple for analogies, green for what the papers say, blue for toolkit output and yellow for caveats. Nothing mathematical rests on a joke, and no joke rests on anything mathematical.
