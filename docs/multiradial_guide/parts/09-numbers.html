9.1Row by row, for \(1 + 8 = 9\)

The algorithm's output, measured, is a sum over the procession. Packet \(j\) contributes the hull of \(j+1\) log-shells, which costs \((j+1)\log d^K\) (the different of \(K\) measures how big a log-shell is), and the theta value \(\underline{q}^{\,j^2}\) acting on it, which contributes \(-\frac{j^2}{2l}\log q\) [Alien, §3.7 (iv); IUTchIV, Thm 1.10, Step (v)].

9.2From the corollary to a height bound

Write the measured upper end in closed form. With \(\sum_{j=1}^{l^*}(j+1) = \frac{l^*(l^*+3)}{2}\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^{l^*} j^2 = \frac{l^*\,l\,(l+1)}{12}\) (both proved in lean/IUT/Multiradial.lean),

\[ -|\log\Theta| \;\lesssim\; \frac{1}{l^*}\sum_{j=1}^{l^*}\Bigl[(j+1)\log d^K - \frac{j^2}{2l}\log q\Bigr] \;=\; \frac{l+5}{4}\,\log d^K \;-\; \frac{l+1}{24}\,\log q . \]

Corollary 3.12 says this is at least \(-|\log q| = -\frac{\log q}{2l}\). Rearranging,

\[ \log q\,\Bigl(\frac{l+1}{24} - \frac{1}{2l}\Bigr) \;\le\; \frac{l+5}{4}\,\log d^K \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \log q \;\le\; \frac{6\,l\,(l+5)}{l^2+l-12}\;\log d^K . \]

For large \(l\) the factor tends to 6, so the inequality reads \(\tfrac{1}{6}\log q \lesssim (1 + O(1/l))\log d^K\). The left side is the height of the curve (the toolkit's \(\mathrm{ht}_E = \tfrac16\log q\)); the right side is controlled by the conductor and the different. That is a Szpiro-type inequality, and through it abc. Before normalizing by \(l^*\), the same computation is Mochizuki's “leading term” \(\frac{l^2}{48}\bigl(6\log d^K - \log q\bigr)\) [Alien, §3.7 (iv)]: “a ‘large positive constant’ \(\frac{l^2}{48}\) times precisely the quantity […] that one wishes to bound from below”. He notes that this computation “was in fact known to the author around the year 2000”, and that a primary motivation for IUT was “establishing a suitable framework, or geometry, in which this computation could be performed.”

9.3The constant \(C_\Theta\) and Theorem 1.10

[IUTchIV] Theorem 1.10 replaces the leading-term heuristic by a fully explicit estimate, including the archimedean places, the places above 2, and all the error terms, and expresses the result as an upper bound \(C_\Theta\) with \(-|\log\Theta| \le C_\Theta\,|\log q|\). The toolkit evaluates it: for \(1 + 8 = 9\) it gets \(C_\Theta = {{ctheta}}\), comfortably \(\ge -1\), and Theorem 1.10's height inequality reads \(\tfrac16\log q = {{thmlhs}} \le {{thmrhs}}\).

How to read these numbers
  • The constants dominate. Theorem 1.10's right-hand side contains \(\eta_{\mathrm{prm}} = 5\cdot10^{20}\) ([ExpEst] §1). For curves this small it swamps everything, so the bound holds trivially and says nothing sharp. The theory is about curves of enormous height, where \(l\) is chosen near \(\sqrt{\text{height}}\) [IUTchIV, Cor. 2.2].
  • \(\log d^K\) is an estimate. The initial Θ-data files record no ramification data for \(K = F(E[l])\), so the toolkit estimates the different and takes \(e = 1\) in the log-shells.
  • Only \(\log q\) is exact. It is computed from the Tate parameters at the odd bad places and cross-checked against an independent computation (stage 7 of the pipeline).
  • The toolkit does not prove anything. It implements the data types and the bookkeeping of the statements. Whether the statements are proved is the subject of §12.
Otaku corner · 体重測定

Sumo wrestlers are weighed before each tournament, and the number is public. Corollary 3.12 is a weigh-in where one wrestler (the Θ-pilot) steps on the scale wearing a heavy keshō-mawashi (the hull) and the other (the q-pilot) in a plain mawashi. The rule says the dressed-up wrestler must weigh at least as much as the plain one. For our four small examples the keshō-mawashi weighs several times more than either wrestler, which is why the rule is satisfied easily. In the big tournaments, curves with height in the millions, the apron is relatively light and the rule starts to bite.
