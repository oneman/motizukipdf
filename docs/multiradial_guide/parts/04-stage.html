4The stage: the log-theta-lattice舞台装置

The algorithm runs on a two-dimensional grid of complete copies of arithmetic, the log-theta-lattice [IUTchIII, Def. 1.4, Def. 3.8 (iii)]. Each node \((n, m)\) is a Θ±ell NF-Hodge theater: one whole copy of “conventional scheme theory” around the elliptic curve, with its number fields, local fields, Galois groups, Frobenioids, and the label symmetries \(\mathbb{F}_l^{\rtimes\pm}\) and \(\mathbb{F}_l^{*}\) (see the Hodge theaters infographic in docs/). Different nodes live in different universes, so nothing in one node is literally an element of another.

4.1Two kinds of arrows

arrowwhat it doeswhat it shares (coric)what it scrambles
log-link ↑At each place \(v\), the \(p\)-adic (or complex) logarithm sends the units of the node below to an additive module that becomes the multiplicative world of the node above. the arithmetic fundamental groups \(\Pi_v\) and \(G_v\) (vertical coricity, [IUTchIII] Thm 1.5 (i)) which elements are units and which are values: it “juggles” \(\boxplus\) and \(\boxtimes\)
Θ-link →Glues the Θ-pilot of \((n,m)\) to the q-pilot of \((n+1,m)\) as abstract monoids. \(G_v\) and the units modulo torsion \(\mathcal{O}^{\times\mu}\) (horizontal coricity, Thm 1.5 (ii)) the ring structure and \(\Pi_v\): the two sides are alien

Two facts about the arrows drive the whole algorithm.

The lattice does not commute. Going up a log-link and then across a Θ-link is not the same as going across and then up. The Lean port in lean/IUT/Link.lean proves this for the toolkit's data model as the theorem square_not_commutative. Consequence: one must use a single Θ-link and do everything else vertically. Using the unit part of one Θ-link and the value part of another would require a commuting square that does not exist [IUTchIII, proof of Cor. 3.12, Step (iii)].

Some data survive both kinds of arrow. The Galois groups \(G_v\), and the units modulo torsion \(\mathcal{O}^{\times\mu}\) rebuilt from them, are shared vertically and horizontally: they are bi-coric [IUTchIII, Thm 1.5 (iii)]. So are the log-shells built from them (Thm 1.5 (iv)). These are the only objects that can serve as a common container for both columns, and Theorem 3.11 uses them exactly that way.

4.2Log-shells: the container that holds the log of everything

For a nonarchimedean place \(v\) over an odd prime \(p\), the log-shell is

\[ \mathcal{I}_v \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2p}\,\log_p\!\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{K_v}^{\times}\bigr) \;\subseteq\; K_v, \]

a compact additive lattice that contains the logarithms of all units, with a little room to spare ([IUTchIII] Def. 1.1, Prop. 1.2). Its purpose is to be an upper bound that is stable under the log-link: whatever level of the column you are on, the logarithm of the units at the next level lands inside the log-shell at this one. That stability is what makes log-shells “mono-analytic containers” [IUTchIII, Rem. 1.5.2], and it is also the reason one of the three blurs, (Ind3), is only an inclusion “\(\subseteq\)” rather than an equality (§7.3). At archimedean places the log-shell is a closed disc and the inclusion becomes a surjection “\(\twoheadrightarrow\)”.

Toolkit caveat

The initial Θ-data JSON files carry no ramification data for the field \(K\). Where \(K_v\) can be written down the toolkit does so: at a Tate-curve place with \(p \nmid 30l\) it is \(\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{60l}, q_v^{1/30l})\) up to an unramified twist, so \(e = 30l/\gcd(30l, \operatorname{ord} q_v)\) and the log-shell has its true log-volume \((1 - 1/e)\log p\). At the places over \(2, 3, 5, l\), where \(K_v\) is wildly ramified, it uses the lower bounds for \(e\) forced by the roots of unity in \(K\), and labels the log-shell a model. The shape of the algorithm is unaffected; the sizes of the containers come in through \(\log d^K\) (§9.3).

Otaku corner · 大道芸

Mochizuki's word for what the log-link does to addition and multiplication is juggling [IUTchIII, Rem. 1.2.2 (vi)]. Picture a daidōgei street performer in Asakusa: two clubs, \(\boxplus\) and \(\boxtimes\), constantly swapping hands. Each throw (log-link) turns the multiplicative club into the additive one. The audience (the coric data) only ever sees the performer's hands, which do not change; the clubs are never in the same hand twice. The log-shell is the performer's reach: whatever is in the air, it is within that circle. The non-commutativity of the lattice is the fact that you cannot juggle and take a bow at the same time.

4.3Frobenius-like and étale-like

Every object in IUT comes in two flavours [IUTchIII, Rem. 1.5.4; Alien, §2.7]:

  • Frobenius-like objects are monoids and Frobenioids that live inside one specific Hodge theater: the actual units, the actual theta values, the actual number field. They are rigid and precise, and they do not travel: they belong to one node of the lattice.
  • Étale-like objects are rebuilt from Galois groups by anabelian algorithms. Because the Galois groups are coric, étale-like objects travel freely along any arrow that shares them. The price is that they are only defined up to the automorphisms of the Galois group.

The bridge between the two is Kummer theory: a Kummer isomorphism identifies a Frobenius-like monoid with its étale-like copy inside Galois cohomology. Mochizuki calls the act of crossing this bridge Kummer-detachment [Alien, §2.7 (vi)]. Crossing it costs an indeterminacy, and that cost is the subject of §7.

Analogy · the cake and the photo of the cake

A Frobenius-like object is a cake in your kitchen. An étale-like object is a photo of the cake, which you can send anywhere. The Kummer isomorphism says “this photo is of that cake”. A photo can be received in another universe, but the receiver can rotate it, crop it, or print it at a different size; they cannot eat it. The multiradial algorithm is the art of sending enough photos, from enough angles, that the receiver can say how much cake there was, in their own units, to within a stated error.
