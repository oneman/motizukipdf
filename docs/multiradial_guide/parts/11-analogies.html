Mochizuki explains the multiradial algorithm through at least six analogies of his own. None of them is a proof and each captures a different facet. Here they are side by side, followed by our less dignified additions.

11.1The Gaussian integral [Alien §1, §3.8]

The classical computation of \(I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}dx\) has no elementary antiderivative to work with. The trick is to take two identical but mutually alien copies of the integral, one in \(x\) and one in \(y\), multiply them, and pass to polar coordinates, where the radial and angular parts decouple:

\[ I^2 = \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} e^{-(x^2+y^2)}\,dx\,dy = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}\,r\,dr\,d\theta = \pi, \qquad I = \sqrt{\pi}. \]

Otaku corner · ネタバレ注意

Spoiler warning for anyone reading [IUTchIII] for the first time: the theta function, the hero of 180 pages, is deliberately forgotten in the last three (“hidden internal structures”). This is the IUT equivalent of a protagonist who saves the world and is then written out of the final episode so the side characters can sign the peace treaty. Mochizuki's justification is the same one a scriptwriter would give: the treaty only needs to know what was saved, not who saved it.
