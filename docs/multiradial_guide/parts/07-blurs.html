7The three blurs: (Ind1), (Ind2), (Ind3)三つのぼかし

An indeterminacy is the price of a descent: whenever the algorithm replaces a rich object by a poorer one that travels better, it must accept every way the poorer object could have come from a rich one. 7.1(Ind1): shuffled seats

Once the data are rebuilt from abstract Galois groups, nothing distinguishes one ordering of the capsule from another, and nothing pins down an isomorphism of \(G_v\) with the Galois group of a specific field. An automorphism of \(G_v\) need not come from a field automorphism (the absolute Galois group of a \(p\)-adic field does not determine the field; see anabelian_guide.pdf), so (Ind1) includes moves that scramble the ring structure. The log-volume does not see them: a procession-normalized log-volume is an average over labels and a sum over places, and both are symmetric.

7.2(Ind2): turning the dial

\(\mathrm{Ism}\) is the group of \(G_v\)-equivariant automorphisms of \(\mathcal{O}^{\times\mu}_{\overline{k}}\) that preserve the lattices \(\mathcal{O}^{\times\mu}_{k_H}\) for every open \(H \subseteq G_v\) [IUTchII, Ex. 1.8 (iv)]. It is compact, so it moves points around while preserving volumes, the way rotating a dial preserves the area of the dial. Its existence is a genuine loss: the units on the two sides of the Θ-link are identified only up to such a twist, and in particular one cannot tell which unit is 1 + p and which is its image under some isometry. Each tensor factor gets its own independent copy, so the twist cannot even be synchronized across labels.

7.3(Ind3): only “fits inside”

Along the 0-column the Kummer isomorphisms at different heights \(m\) are compatible with the log-links only in the sense that the log-shell at one level contains the image of the units from the next. Mochizuki calls this upper semi-compatibility (Theorem 3.11 (ii), Prop. 3.5 (ii)). It is the price of the “vertical shift” that the argument needs: the units must be taken one level below the value groups they measure, and vertically coric containers are the only way to accommodate that [IUTchIII, proof of Cor. 3.12, Step (iv)]. In terms of log-volume, an inclusion can only make things bigger, so (Ind3) converts the would-be equality into an inequality from above. The log-volumes themselves are compatible with the log-links on the nose (Prop. 3.9 (iv)).

Why the blurs are harmless for log-volumes · [IUTchIII] proof of Cor 3.12, Step (x)

The three indeterminacies describe “some sort of ‘formal quotient’, like the ‘fine moduli spaces’ that appear in algebraic geometry. […] [T]he procession-normalized mono-analytic log-volumes […] furnish a means of constructing a sort of associated ‘coarse space’ […] in the sense that […] the resulting log-volumes ∈ ℝ are invariant with respect to the indeterminacies (Ind1), (Ind2), and have the effect of converting the indeterminacy (Ind3) into an inequality [from above].”

7.4The invisible indeterminacies

Two more blurs exist but can be ignored “essentially as a formal consequence of the way in which the various objects that appear are defined” [Alien, §3.7 (i); IUTchIII, Rem. 3.11.4]: multiplication by roots of unity (the only overlap between theta values and local units, by non-interference, and the theta values are stable under it), and the sign \(\pm1\) in the cyclotomic rigidity for κ-coric functions (the global Frobenioids only use all of \(F_{\mathrm{mod}}^\times\), which is stable under inversion).

Analogy · Shinkansen reserved seating

Think of the procession as a Shinkansen car with reserved seats. (Ind1): the conductor reshuffles who sits in which seat. (Ind2): every passenger rotates in their seat (they can, the seats swivel). (Ind3): the luggage rack is “at least as big as” the luggage, not exactly as big. Ask how much the car weighs: the first two do not change the answer, and the third means the scale can only say “at most this much”. The train still leaves on time, to the second.

Otaku corner · 三人組

Every long-running comedy has a trio of troublemakers who undo the protagonist's careful plans. IUT's trio are (Ind1), (Ind2) and (Ind3). Mochizuki insists they are “relatively mild”; readers have long asked whether one of them is quietly carrying the plot. [EssLgc] §3.11 even ranks them, “(Ind3 > 1 + 2)”: once the output is stable under (Ind3), stability under the other two comes almost for free. The equivalent in a shōnen manga is the reveal that the quiet one was the strongest all along.
