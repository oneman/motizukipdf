2Why this is hard: the Θ-link breaks the ring環が壊れる理由

A ring has two operations, \(+\) and \(\times\), welded together by the distributive law. [Alien] §2.11 calls them the “two underlying combinatorial dimensions of a ring”, and the image to keep in mind is a sheet of paper: multiplication runs one way, addition the other, and the distributive law is what keeps the sheet from tearing. For a local field \(k\) the multiplicative direction itself splits again into a unit part \(\mathcal{O}_k^\times\) (things of size 1) and a value group part (the powers of a uniformizer, which carry the size).

The map we want, \(\underline{q}^{\,j^2} \mapsto \underline{q}\), only makes sense on the value-group part. It is a perfectly good map of multiplicative monoids, but there is no ring homomorphism behind it: in characteristic zero \((a + b)^N \ne a^N + b^N\), so any map that dilates sizes by \(N\) tears the sheet. That is why the Θ-link cannot be a map of rings, of schemes, or of anything that remembers \(+\). It is a gluing of two separate copies of arithmetic along the part that has no \(+\) in it.

Analogy · characteristic \(p\) has it easy

In characteristic \(p\) the map \(x \mapsto x^p\) is a ring homomorphism (Frobenius), because \((a+b)^p = a^p + b^p\) there. So over function fields the N-th power trick of §1.1 can be run inside one copy of arithmetic, and the geometric Szpiro inequality has proofs along exactly these lines (via Frobenius, or via the Kodaira–Spencer map) [Alien, §2.4–§2.9]. Number fields have no Frobenius. IUT's move is to build a substitute for Frobenius that is allowed to leave the universe: the Θ-link. Mochizuki's 2026 report gives the simplest model: “when \(N = p\), working modulo \(p\) […] there is a common ring structure that is compatible with the \(p\)-th power map” [Form26, §3]. Over \(\mathbb{Z}\) there is no such common ring structure, so the theory has to manufacture a common container instead.

2.1What goes through the Θ-link and what does not

So after the Θ-link we have a strange situation. On the right there is an honest elliptic curve over an honest number field, with its q-pilot. On the left there is another honest elliptic curve over another honest number field, with its Θ-pilot. And there is a bridge between them that carries only abstract groups and multiplicative monoids. The question of Theorem 3.11 is:

The question Theorem 3.11 answers

Can the person standing on the right describe the Θ-pilot of the left in their own ring theory, using only what came across the bridge? If so, how precisely?

Answer: yes, up to three explicit blurs, and the description is good enough to measure. The tools that make this possible are exactly the ones developed in the decade before IUT: anabelian geometry (rebuild a ring-like object from its Galois group alone), Kummer theory (relate an object to its portable photograph in Galois cohomology), the étale theta function (whose Kummer theory has an unusually rigid cyclotome), and the \(p\)-adic logarithm (which turns the multiplicative units into an additive module, and so manufactures a place where addition can live again). The companion guide anabelian_guide.pdf in this repository covers the first three in depth.

Otaku corner · 異世界転生

Every isekai protagonist faces the problem of Theorem 3.11. You are hit by a truck (Truck-kun, the genre's most prolific transport mechanism) and wake up in a world whose laws of nature differ from yours. All you carried across is what fits in your pockets: in IUT, an abstract group \(G_v\) and a bag of units. The ring structure, your house, the power grid, stayed behind. The plot of a hundred light novels is then: rebuild modern civilization from what you carried, in a way that also works in the new world. IUT's version is stricter than any light novel: what you rebuild must be described so that a native of either world would accept it, and you have to say exactly how wrong it might be. No cheat skill (チート能力) is granted, although the étale theta function comes close.

2.2Why not just compare the two sides directly?

Because there is nothing to compare them in. To say “the Θ-pilot on the left has log-volume \({{thetapilot}}\) and the q-pilot on the right has log-volume \({{qpilot}}\)” one needs a common copy of the real numbers into which both log-volumes map, and a common ring structure in which both pilots are honest arithmetic line bundles. The Θ-link provides neither: it identifies the two pilots only as abstract objects of a monoid, up to isomorphism class. Any argument of the form “compute both sides and compare” silently assumes a common ring structure, which is precisely what the Θ-link destroyed.

Mochizuki's 2026 report puts it as a diagram [Form26, §3]: for two ring structures \({}^\dagger R\) and \({}^\ddagger R\) glued along their multiplicative monoids, the multiradial representation is a common container

\[ {}^\dagger R \;\subseteq\; \bigl(\text{multiradial representation of the Θ-pilot}\bigr) \;\supseteq\; {}^\ddagger R, \]

built from “the multiplicative monoid and abstract group structures (but not the ring structures!)” that are “common (cf. ‘logical AND ∧!’) to †, ‡”. The rest of this guide is about how that container is built and what it costs.
