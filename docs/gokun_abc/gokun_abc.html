The Gokun Strategy Guide

We read Go Yamashita's {{total}}-page survey A proof of the abc conjecture after Mochizuki and marked our own homework against it: what it taught us, what it confirmed, and what in this repository it shows to be wrong or sloppy. With figures, real numbers, and red-pen stamps. （山下サーベイで答え合わせ：攻略本を読んでみた）

Paper: version of June 25, 2024, now in pdf/ · examples: the {{nex}} sets of initial Θ-data the toolkit builds exactly · every example number comes from iut pipeline · November 2026
Abstract   Until now this project read Mochizuki in the original: four IUT papers, [Alien], [EssLgc]. Yamashita's survey [Y] is the 攻略本, the thick strategy guide you buy after getting stuck on the same boss for a week. It re-proves the whole route from anabelian geometry to the abc inequality in one self-contained document, with its own notation and with the elementary computations written out in full. Reading it produced three kinds of result. Corrections: four places where our toolkit or earlier articles say something [Y] shows to be wrong or empty (§2). Confirmations: eight conventions we had chosen that match (§2). New material: seven ideas that none of our earlier write-ups contain, including the “miracle identity”, the reason the log-theta-lattice needs infinite columns, and a short proof that the obvious cheat code \(\Theta^N\) cannot work (§3–§9). The four corrections have since been made in the toolkit and in funfun_iut.pdf; §10 says what changed.

1The book on the table机の上の一冊

The file that arrived was the text layer of abc2024Jun25.pdf. The same document is public on the author's RIMS page, so the PDF itself, {{total}} pages, is what went into pdf/. Its first line of content is the abstract, which is two sentences long. The remaining {{total}} pages are the fine print.

The author's e-mail handle is gokun, and we will make the obvious joke exactly once: this is a man who went to train under a master on a remote mountain (Kyoto has several), ran an intensive seminar on the technique from May to November 2013, and came back with a {{total}}-page scroll. The acknowledgments thank the executives of Toyota Central R&D Labs for “a special position that enabled him to concentrate on his research in pure mathematics”, and thank Sakichi Toyoda, who died in 1930, for the philanthropic culture behind it. This makes [Y] the only survey of the abc conjecture with an automotive sponsor, and explains its method, which is kaizen: no single step is hard, there are simply a great many of them and none is skipped.

The introduction opens with a story attributed to Grothendieck: there are two ways to crack a nut. One is a nutcracker. The other is to soak it in water “for an extended period of time until its shell dissolves naturally”. Japanese cuisine has a third way, which is to bury it in rice bran for three years and call the result 糠漬け, and that is closer to what happens here. Yamashita then says something none of our earlier write-ups dared to:

“…these numerous trivial constructions, whose exposition occupies literally hundreds of pages, allow one to conclude a highly nontrivial consequence … practically effortlessly!”

and, a paragraph later, that if one sets aside the delicate way the pieces are combined, “the only nontrivial mathematical ingredient in inter-universal Teichmüller theory is the classical result [pGC], which was already known in the last century”. [pGC] is Mochizuki's 1999 theorem on the pro-\(p\) anabelian geometry of curves, the subject of our anabelian_guide.pdf. In anime terms the survey is describing a slice-of-life series: for eleven episodes the cast makes tea, labels prime-strips and walks to school, and then in episode twelve somebody says one sentence and you find you are crying. The sentence is Corollary 13.13. The tea is §2–§12.

3The route has three warp pipes we skippedワープ土管

Our earlier description was: take \(a + b = c\), form the Frey curve, apply IUT. The survey's §1 shows that this compresses three separate reductions, each of which changes what object the theory is applied to.

First pipe (Cor. 0.2 from Thm. 0.1). abc is the case \(X = \mathbb{P}^1\), \(D = \{0,1,\infty\}\), \(d = 1\) of Vojta's conjecture for curves, evaluated at the rational point \(-a/b\). The proof is a dozen lines and uses nothing but \(|a + b| \le 2\max\{|a|,|b|\}\). This is the tutorial level.

Second pipe (Prop. 1.2, from [GenEll]). To prove Vojta for every hyperbolic curve it is enough to prove it for \(\mathbb{P}^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}\), and only on a compactly bounded subset: points that stay a fixed distance from the three cusps at finitely many chosen places. The tool is a non-critical Belyi map (Appendix C.2), a map to \(\mathbb{P}^1\) branched only over \(\{0,1,\infty\}\) that sends one chosen finite set of points to the cusps while keeping another chosen finite set away from them. This is the step that matters for us. A high-quality abc triple is, by its nature, a point close to a cusp at some place; the theory is not asked to handle it head-on. The Belyi map carries the problem somewhere else first, like the green pipe that drops you in a different world with the same coin count.

Third pipe (Prop. 1.15). On a compactly bounded subset whose support contains the place 2 and the archimedean place, every point outside a finite set comes, after enlarging the field as in §2, from initial Θ-data. The archimedean place and 2 are awkward for the theory, and this is how they are neutralised: the height contribution there is bounded by a constant \(C_{\mathcal K}\) depending only on the subset. That is why \(\mathbb{V}^{\mathrm{bad}}_{\mathrm{mod}}\) excludes 2 (correction one), and why nobody minds.

Only then does the fourth box, the one all the excitement is about, get used. Yamashita is blunt about the first three (Remark 1.15.2): the inequality \(-|\log(q)| \le -|\log(\Theta)|\) “is almost a tautological translation of the inequality which we want to show”, and “no deep things happen in these reduction steps”.

4The level cap: our examples are below itレベル不足

Proposition 1.15 does not say the inequality holds for every elliptic curve. It says there is a finite exceptional set \(\mathrm{Exc}_{\mathcal K,d,\epsilon}\), enlarged several times during the proof, outside which it holds. Two of the enlargements are explicit. Writing \(h = \log(q^{\forall})\) and \(d^* = 2^{12}\cdot3^3\cdot5\cdot d\):

  • (s1) all curves with \(h^{1/2} < \xi_{\mathrm{prm}} + \eta_{\mathrm{prm}}\) are thrown into Exc, where \(\xi_{\mathrm{prm}} \ge 5\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{prm}}\) come from the prime number theorem;
  • the last step throws in all curves with \(\epsilon_E := (60 d^*)^2\, h^{-1/2}\log(2 d^* h) > \min\{1, \epsilon\}\).

[Y] keeps \(\eta_{\mathrm{prm}}\) abstract. Our toolkit plugs in \(5\cdot10^{20}\), citing [ExpEst]. With that value and \(d = 1\):

{{table:level}}

So the toolkit's old “holds” was true and said nothing: the right-hand side it compares against contains \(20(e^*_{\mathrm{mod}}l + \eta_{\mathrm{prm}}) \approx 10^{22}\), and the “\(\epsilon\)” in \((1+\epsilon)\) is at best {{eps_min}}. This is correction three. It is not a defect of the theory, which only ever promised a statement about all but finitely many curves; it was a defect of our printout, which presented a comparison with a twenty-two-digit constant as a result. One precision, which the first version of this article blurred: the exceptional set belongs to Prop. 1.15 (Cor. 2.2). Prop. 1.12 (Thm. 1.10) is stated for every initial Θ-data, and for our curves it is true for the dull reason that its constant term alone exceeds the left side. The toolkit now says both things in so many words.

5The miracle identity奇跡の等式

Why is the constant in front of \((\text{log-diff} + \text{log-cond})\) exactly 1, and not 2, or 6, or \(\pi\)? None of our earlier write-ups asked. Remark 1.15.3 answers, and Yamashita names it himself: the miracle identity.

The bound on the Θ-pilot (Lemma 1.10) has two competing terms. The theta values \(\underline{q}^{j^2}\) shrink the region: averaged over \(j = 1,\dots,l^*\) they contribute \(-\frac{l+1}{24}\log q\). The log-shells that hold them grow it: packet \(j\) has \(j + 1\) tensor factors, each costing one \(\log\mathfrak{d}^K\), and the average is \(\frac{l+5}{4}\log\mathfrak{d}^K\). Writing the height as \(\frac16\log q\),

\[ -|\log(\Theta)| \;\le\; \underbrace{\frac{l+1}{4}}_{\text{quadratic, averaged}}\Bigl(-\tfrac16\log q\Bigr) \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{l+5}{4}}_{\text{linear, averaged}}\log\mathfrak{d}^K \;+\; \cdots \]

and the two coefficients agree to leading order in \(l\). Yamashita factors each into its causes:

\[ \underbrace{6}_{\lambda\text{-line over } j\text{-line}} \times \underbrace{\tfrac12}_{\text{theta lives on a double cover}} \times \underbrace{\tfrac1{2^2}}_{\text{exponent } j^2} \times \underbrace{\tfrac13}_{\sum j^2 \approx n^3/3} \;=\; \underbrace{\tfrac1{2}}_{\text{differents are linear}} \times \underbrace{\tfrac12}_{\sum j \approx n^2/2}. \] {{table:miracle}}

The last two columns are a check on us: the survey's closed form and the number the toolkit computes by summing over \(j\) agree to six decimals for all four examples (the build fails if they do not).

Two further remarks in [Y] are worth recording. First, the identity was already visible in Mochizuki's Hodge–Arakelov theory of the late 1990s (Appendix A.3), years before there was any framework in which it could be used: “Mochizuki firstly observed this equality, and next he established the framework … in which these calculations work.” Second, the 1 is sharp. Masser showed by analytic number theory that the main term cannot be improved [Mass2], so whatever a proof of abc looks like, it has to land on this coefficient exactly.

6Why the lattice needs infinite columns縦は無限

Our articles say the log-theta-lattice “does not commute” and cite [IUTchIII] Remark 1.4.1. The opening of [Y] §13 gives the reason in six characters: \(\log(a^N) \ne (\log a)^N\). The Θ-link is, on value groups, a power map. The log-link is a logarithm. They do not commute for the same reason they do not commute on a pocket calculator.

This is the cleanest statement we have seen of why the theory needs the whole vertical line and not just one step up, and it explains the shape of the three indeterminacies. One wants to see the additive structure on the far side of a Θ-link, which only carries multiplicative data. A log-link converts one to the other, but breaks the square. Using the full column repairs the square at the cost of no longer knowing which row you are on, and that ignorance is precisely the vertical indeterminacy, (Indet ↑), our (Ind3).

The survey also says, concretely, how big the indeterminacies are allowed to be in the final estimate. In Lemma 1.9 the region whose volume is bounded is the holomorphic hull of the union of \(\phi\bigl(\underline{q}^{j^2}\,\mathcal{I}\bigr)\) over all \(\mathbb{Q}_p\)-linear automorphisms \(\phi\) of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\otimes\mathcal{I}\) that preserve the tensor-packet log-shell \(\mathcal{I}\). Our guide described (Ind2) as a compact group of isometries and (Ind1) as label permutations. Both are true, and both are swallowed in the computation by this far cruder group: anything that maps the lattice to itself. The estimate does not need to know what the indeterminacies are, only that they stay inside the log-shell. Remark 1.6.1 puts the underlying point memorably: once \(\frac1p\mathfrak{m}_k\) is regarded as a bare \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-module, nothing intrinsic distinguishes \(\frac1p\varpi, \frac1p\varpi^2, \dots, \frac1p\varpi^e\), “a kind of differentiation over \(\mathbb{F}_1\)”.

Otaku corner · 壁

Yamashita calls the Θ-link and the log-link “walls”, and says Frobenius-like objects are what you build walls from while étale-like objects are what can “penetrate these walls” (§0.1, §4.3). A long-running series about a city behind three concentric walls taught a generation what happens next: the interesting characters are the ones who can get through, and the plot is about what, exactly, is on the other side. Here the answer is “another copy of the same city, with the addition table missing”.

7The banned cheat code \(\Theta^N\)禁止コマンド

The Θ-link identifies \(\{\underline{q}^{j^2}\}_{1\le j\le l^*}\) on one side with \(\underline{q}\) on the other. An obvious question, which Remark 13.13.3 asks and none of our write-ups did: why those exponents? If replacing \(\underline q\) by \(\underline q^{j^2}\) wins a factor of about \(l^2\), replace the theta function by its \(N\)-th power and win \(N\) times more.

variant of the Θ-linkworks?why (Remark 13.13.3)
\(\{\underline{q}^{j^2}\} \mapsto \underline{q}^{\lambda}\), \(\lambda > 0\)yes, pointlessly The left side of the final inequality becomes \(\lambda\cdot 0\). For \(\lambda \ll l\) nothing changes; for \(\lambda > l\) the result is weaker.
\(\{(\underline{q}^{j^2})^N\} \mapsto \underline{q}\), \(N > 1\)no All three rigidities of the mono-theta environment fail. (a) Cyclotomic rigidity uses that the commutator of the theta group is exactly quadratic. (b) Constant-multiple rigidity fails: the \(N\)-th power map relates non-isomorphic line bundles. (c) \(\Theta^N\) has a zero of order \(N\) at the cusps, so each loop in the log-Kummer correspondence multiplies by \(N\) and it “totally collapses”.
\(\underline{q} \mapsto \underline{q}^{\lambda}\) with no theta functionno The splitting that makes Kummer theory compatible sends \(\Theta \mapsto 1\) at the zero-labelled point. The Kummer class of \(\underline q\) is never trivial, so there is nothing to split along.

The second row has a second, independent proof that it must fail, and it is the best paragraph in the remark. If \(\Theta^N\) worked, the same computation would give

\[ \operatorname{ht} \;\lesssim\; \frac{1+\epsilon}{N}\,\bigl(\text{log-diff} + \text{log-cond}\bigr), \qquad N > 1, \]

which contradicts Masser's lower bound. The theory is therefore under an external constraint: its indeterminacies must be exactly as large as they are. A version of IUT that proved more would be wrong. In this sense the three rigidities of §7 of the survey are not technical conveniences; they are what stops the machine from proving a false theorem.

Boke: “If squaring the exponent is good, why not raise the whole theta function to the power \(N\)?” Tsukkomi, with fan: “Because then abc would be false, Masser proved that in 1990, and also your Heisenberg group stopped being Heisenberg.” なんでやねん。

8Saying goodbye to scheme theoryさよならスキーム論

Appendix A is five pages and is where we would now send anyone who asks what IUT is for. It tells the origin story in three comparison theorems: de Rham (periods like \(2\pi i\)), \(p\)-adic Hodge, and Mochizuki's Hodge–Arakelov theory, which evaluates sections of a line bundle at the \(l\)-torsion points and finds “Gaussian poles” \(q^{j^2/8l}\) on the étale side. Then A.4 makes an argument by wishful thinking.

Suppose \(E[l]\) had a global multiplicative subspace, a rank-one submodule that is the canonical \(\mu_l\) at every bad place at once. Then Hodge–Arakelov theory would give an inclusion of arithmetic line bundles \(\underline{q}\,\mathcal{O}_K \hookrightarrow \underline{q}^{j^2}\mathcal{O}_K\), and comparing degrees,

\[ 0 \;\lesssim\; -(\text{large number}) \;\approx\; -\operatorname{ht}. \]

So heights would be bounded, so there would be only finitely many elliptic curves, which is false. Hence no such subspace exists in general. A normal mathematician stops here. Yamashita's description of what Mochizuki did instead is the sentence of the paper:

“Instead, we respect the inclusion \(\underline{q}\,\mathcal{O}_K \hookrightarrow \underline{q}^{j^2}\mathcal{O}_K\), and we say a good-bye to the scheme theory.”

The Θ-link is that inclusion, declared as an identification between two different scheme theories. It is not a contradiction because degrees in different scheme theories cannot be compared. The multiradial algorithm is what lets them be compared after all, at a price, and “we can calculate that the indeterminacies are (roughly) log-diff + log-cond”. Hence \(0 \lesssim -\operatorname{ht} + \text{log-diff} + \text{log-cond}\). In the same breath, the definition of initial Θ-data is reinterpreted: \(\underline C_K\) and the cusp \(\underline\epsilon\) “behave as though they are a global multiplicative subspace and a canonical generator up to \(\{\pm1\}\)” (after Def. 10.1). Our toolkit stores \(\underline\epsilon\) as the integer 1. It is in fact the ghost of an object that cannot exist.

Two more ideas from the survey's closing remarks, neither of which we had:

  • Hyperbolicity of number fields (Remark 13.13.2). In \(p\)-adic Teichmüller theory the derivative of the canonical Frobenius lifting gives \((1-p)(2g-2) \le 0\), which says the curve is hyperbolic. The two ways of computing the \(q\)-pilot's log-volume are, by analogy, a self-intersection “\(\Delta.\Delta\)” of the diagonal in \(\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_1}\mathbb{Z}\), and \(|\log(\Theta)| \le |\log(q)| \approx 0\) “means the hyperbolicity of number fields”. The dictionary (end of §3.5): number field ↔ hyperbolic curve in characteristic \(p\); log-link ↔ Frobenius; Θ-link ↔ the lifting \(p^n/p^{n+1} \rightsquigarrow p^{n+1}/p^{n+2}\); the whole lattice ↔ a canonical lift of Frobenius.
  • Descent to \(\mathbb{F}_1\) (Remark 13.13.1). The Θ-link behaves like an “absolute Frobenius over \(\mathbb{Z}\)”, and the three indeterminacies can be read as “descent data from \(\mathbb{Z}\) to \(\mathbb{F}_1\)”. The survey calls the multiradial algorithm “a partial (meaningful) realisation of the philosophy of ‘the field of one element’ \(\mathbb{F}_1\)”.
Otaku corner · 卒業

Japanese school years end in March with a graduation ceremony, a song everyone cries through, and the second button of somebody's uniform. “We say a good-bye to the scheme theory” is a graduation speech. Scheme theory was a good school. It taught us étale cohomology and how to behave around a base change. But the inclusion we want to respect lives off campus, and so, bowing once to Grothendieck-sensei, we walk out through the gate into a universe with no ring structure and a part-time job computing log-volumes.

9What the survey does not claim書いていないこと

A strategy guide is also useful for what it leaves out. Four things:

Uniform abc is not proved (Remark 1.15.5). The bound depends on the degree \(d\) of the field. The survey extracts the dependence the proof gives, \(\operatorname{ht} \le H_{\mathrm{unif}}\cdot\min\{1,\epsilon\}^{-3} \epsilon_d^{-3}\, d^{\,4+\epsilon_d} + H_{\mathcal K}\), and says plainly that uniformity in \(d\) “is not proved yet”.

The \(\epsilon\)-term is only bounded, and hints at something (Remark 1.15.4). Along the way the proof gives \(\operatorname{ht} \le \delta + *\,\delta^{1/2}\log\delta\) with \(\delta = \text{log-diff} + \text{log-cond}\). Yamashita: “It seems that the exponent \(\frac12\) suggests a possible relation to Riemann hypothesis.” This is offered as a suggestion, not a result.

No explicit constants. \(\eta_{\mathrm{prm}}\), \(\xi_{\mathrm{prm}}\), \(C_{\mathcal K}\) and the size of Exc stay abstract. The level gauge of §4 uses the toolkit's value for \(\eta_{\mathrm{prm}}\), which comes from elsewhere.

No engagement with the dispute. The author ran seminars on IUT in 2013 and this version is dated June 2024; it proves Corollary 13.13 in under a page, by saying that after admitting the three indeterminacies the two columns' étale-like objects are isomorphic and that “the holomorphic hull of the union of possible images of Θ-pilot objects … contains a region which is isomorphic (not equal) to the region determined by the q-pilot objects”. The words “isomorphic (not equal)” are exactly where the disagreement described in multiradial_guide.pdf §12 lives: Scholze and Stix argue the step has no content once copies are identified; Mochizuki argues that identifying them is the error; Joshi argues a needed property is unproved; the LANA formalisation reported in July 2026 that it had not yet reconstructed the required compatibility. [Y] neither mentions nor answers any of this. It is a faithful and much more readable account of what the proof says, and for that question it leaves the reader where [IUTchIII] does. We are still a toolkit, not a referee. 審判ではありません。

Mochizuki numbers his indeterminacies like a bureaucrat: (Ind1), (Ind2), (Ind3). Yamashita draws them as arrows: ↶↷, →, ↑. Anyone who grew up in a 1990s arcade recognises the second notation immediately. It is a special-move input.
